# The banned list, with replacements

The fix is almost never a synonym. Delete the phrase and see if the sentence still works; if it
does not, say the specific thing the phrase was standing in for.

## Openers

| Banned | Do instead |
|---|---|
| In today's fast-paced world / digital age | Start with the point or the problem. |
| In the ever-evolving landscape of ... | Delete. |
| In the realm of ... / When it comes to ... | Name the thing: "For PRDs, ..." |
| Let's dive in / Let's dive deep / Let's explore / Buckle up | Delete. The next heading starts it. |
| Picture this / Imagine a world where | Tell the real scene in past tense. |
| Have you ever wondered ... | State the answer. |
| ... is a multifaceted / complex discipline that ... | Delete the definition paragraph; answer the title. |
| Whether you're a seasoned PM or just starting out | Delete. Write for one reader. |
| As a product manager, you know ... | Delete, or state the fact. |
| We've all been there | Delete, or tell when the author was there. |

## Closers

| Banned | Do instead |
|---|---|
| In conclusion / To sum up / In summary / To wrap up | End on the usable thing and the way on. |
| At the end of the day / Ultimately, | Delete. |
| By following these steps, you'll ... | Delete. |
| Happy designing! / Good luck! / I hope this helps | Delete. |
| Now it's your turn | Link the next piece instead. |

## Throat-clearing

| Banned | Do instead |
|---|---|
| It's important to note that / It's worth noting / It's worth mentioning | Delete; state the fact. |
| Needless to say / It goes without saying | Then do not say it, or just say it. |
| The fact of the matter is / Simply put / Basically | Delete. |
| That being said / With that in mind / Having said that | "But", or nothing. |
| In order to | "To". |
| Due to the fact that | "Because". |
| A wide range of / a variety of / various / a myriad of / a plethora of | Give the number or the list. |

## Hype words

game-changer, game-changing, revolutionary, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, seamless,
seamlessly, robust, powerful, leverage (verb; use "use"), unlock, unleash, supercharge, turbocharge,
elevate, empower, harness, transformative, holistic, synergy, best-in-class, world-class,
next-level, effortless, delve, tapestry, navigate (use "work through", "handle", or the real verb),
journey (use "process", or name the stages), landscape, realm, paradigm, ecosystem (for a product),
crucial, vital, pivotal, essential, key (filler adjective), invaluable, actionable insights,
data-driven (without data), deep dive, comprehensive guide, ultimate guide, "a testament to",
"stands as", "serves as" (use "is").

## Hedges and intensifiers

can help you (say what it does), may potentially, arguably, very, really, truly, incredibly,
extremely, highly, actually, literally, in many ways, to some extent, quite, rather, somewhat.
Keep a hedge only when the evidence is honestly mixed, and say what it is mixed about.

## Structural tells

- **Warm-up paragraph** that defines the topic. Cut it; the first paragraph answers the title.
- **Restating summary** at the end. Cut it.
- **Generic tip lists** (seven or ten items that fit any company). Replace with three to five
  claims the author would defend, each with a case.
- **Reflex triads**: "clear, concise and compelling"; "find, evaluate and onboard". Keep the one
  word that is true.
- **Mirrored pairs**: "It's not about X, it's about Y." "Less X, more Y." "X isn't just Y. It's Z."
  Say Y.
- **Dash chains**: more than one em dash in a paragraph. Use periods, colons or parentheses.
- **Same-shape sections**: every section claim, three bullets, wrap-up line. Vary by content.
- **Bold for emphasis** inside sentences. Bold is for labels only.
- **Emoji** in headings or lists.
- **Every heading a question.** Questions only where they are the reader's real question.
- **Colon-gerund titles**: "Mastering Roadmaps: A Comprehensive Guide". Use the plain claim or the
  search phrase: "How to build a roadmap people use".
- **Fake balance**: "Both have pros and cons; it depends on your needs." Make the call and say
  when the other option wins.
- **Self-answered questions**: "The result? Chaos." Say "The result was two missed releases."
- **Announcement phrases**: "Here's the thing", "Here's why", "The kicker", "Spoiler alert",
  "Let that sink in", "Read that again".
- **Exclamation marks.** None.
- **Invented specifics**: a client, a percentage or a story the author does not have. Mark the gap
  `[OWNER: ...]` instead.
