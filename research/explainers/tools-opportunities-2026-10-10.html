Ten tools for launch, and seventy-two more

These are the ten small tools to build first: four for designers and researchers, four for product owners and delivery, two for metrics. Each one does a job people repeat every sprint, study or quarter, and each pastes into Jira, Figma or a spreadsheet. Another 72 ideas wait behind them. One paid check, about $0.23, would put a search number on the phrases the store does not cover yet.

October 10, 2026. Research only: nothing on this page is built yet. No paid calls were made.

Think of the tools as the calculator in a teacher's drawer: small, used every week, and kept because it gives the right answer fast.

1. The one-screen version

  • The ten: SUS score calculator, user story builder, backlog prioritization scorer, sprint capacity calculator, usability test sample size, delivery forecast, retention and churn calculator, type scale generator, survey sample size and margin of error, OKR checker.
  • Why these: each scored highest on repeat use, then on search demand and on how open Google’s first page is. Each also sits in a category we write about at launch.
  • What beats the AI answer: on all 12 calculator searches we pulled on October 8, Google’s AI Overview offered to do the maths itself. A one-line formula loses to that. A tool that takes a pasted list, draws a chart or exports to Jira does not.
  • The store is thin here. It was built for articles, so most tool phrases have no volume yet. The ones it has are strong: user story template (2,900), PRD template (2,900), sample size calculator (8,100), retention rate formula (1,600), OKR examples (1,300).
  • Every tool page has the working tool on top, then what it is, when to use it, how it works, a worked example and links to the articles beside it. Google treats a tool page as real content only when the tool works and the page explains it.

2. The launch ten

Read each card as a promise to one person: here is the job, here is what you type, here is what you get to paste.

  1. 1

    SUS score calculator

    UX research · about 6 hours · pastes as: Spreadsheet (paste in, CSV out)
    For
    UX researcher, product designer
    The job
    Score a System Usability Scale study and say what the number means
    You type
    Paste the 10 answers per person from a spreadsheet (one row each)
    You get
    SUS score, grade and percentile against the 68 average, a confidence interval, per-item averages, flags for straight-lined rows
    Why they come back
    Every usability round ends with a SUS score; teams re-run it each release to compare
    Closest free tool today
    Trymata free SUS calculator; Lyssna Google Sheets template. Ours: Paste a whole sheet instead of typing 10 boxes per person; confidence interval and straight-lining check that the free ones skip.
    Search
    sus score calculator; system usability scale calculator; how to calculate sus score (no store data)

    The most open tool page we have seen: on page one Nielsen Norman Group ranks with an article and only one or two real calculators appear. Researchers use it after every round.

3. The second ten, for the drip

One or two a month after launch, in this order. Seven smaller ideas fold into these and the launch ten as extra modes, so they never need pages of their own.

  1. PRD outline generator (Product discovery, score 84). Start a product requirements document with the right sections for the size of the work. PRD template, 2,900 at KD 6. Template-shaped, so it ships beside the first templates.
  2. Palette contrast checker (Accessibility, score 78). Check every text and background pair in a palette against WCAG at once, and get the nearest passing color. First reserve for launch: swap it in if you want accessibility on day one.
  3. Microcopy length checker (UX design, score 80). Check button labels, errors and toasts against length limits and plain-language rules. No dedicated tool found; a clear gap for UX writers.
  4. Positioning statement builder (Product marketing, score 80). Write a positioning statement from the parts that make it up. Positioning statement, 2,900 at KD 12; no generator found.
  5. NPS calculator with margin of error (Product metrics, score 78). Compute Net Promoter Score and say whether a change between two surveys is real. Adds the 'is this change real' check; CSAT and CES as a second tab.
  6. Unit economics: LTV, CAC and payback (Growth and pricing, score 78). See whether each customer pays back what it cost to win. Customer lifetime value and acquisition cost, 4,400 each; the founder's page.
  7. Spacing scale and token export (Design systems, score 78). Pick a spacing scale and export it as tokens. Design system leads; W3C token format.
  8. Tint and shade scale generator (UI design, score 76). Make a 50 to 950 color ramp from one brand color, with contrast labels on each step. Pairs with the contrast checker: ramps with contrast labels.
  9. A/B test sample size and duration planner (Product metrics, score 72). Find how many users and how many days a test needs before you start it. Power analysis sample size calculator, 880 at KD 7; significance check as a second tab.
  10. Survey question checker (UX research, score 74). Catch leading, loaded and double-barreled questions before a survey goes out. Product survey questions, 1,600 at KD 8; rule-based, explained flags.

4. What one tool page holds

The tool comes first and works without a sign-up. The teaching comes under it.

Every page follows the search-policy rulebook. The tool must work, and the page must explain when and how to use it; a calculator on an empty page is thin. The page loads fast and works on a phone, nothing covers the tool, and no tool sends queries to Google. Results are computed in the browser: nothing the reader pastes leaves the page.

Rule One tool, one page, one URL. Modes live on the page as tabs, never as separate pages.

6. The people and their small jobs

Fourteen kinds of reader, and the moments in their week where a small tool saves a task. The ideas came from these lists, not from the keyword store.

Product manager

Owns the why and the what; lives between research, design, engineering and the business.

  • Ranking a long list of ideas before quarterly planning
  • Writing OKRs and checking they are outcomes, not tasks
  • Starting a PRD for a new initiative
  • Telling a stakeholder when a feature will ship, with a range
  • Reading an A/B test result before the review meeting
  • Working out the retention or churn number for a monthly review
  • Turning a list of tickets into release notes
  • Putting a cost on a recurring meeting to argue it away
  • Placing stakeholders on a power and interest grid at kick-off

Product owner

Runs the backlog with the team, story by story.

  • Writing user stories with acceptance criteria several times a week
  • Sorting a release scope into Must, Should, Could and Won't
  • Converting a backlog in points into sprints and dates
  • Checking a story against INVEST before refinement
  • Turning acceptance criteria into a test list for QA
  • Preparing capacity for sprint planning with the scrum master

Product designer

Designs the flows and screens and tests them with people.

  • Deciding how many people to recruit for a usability test
  • Scoring a SUS questionnaire after the test
  • Building a type scale for a new product
  • Checking a palette for contrast before handoff
  • Checking tap targets on a mobile design
  • Writing a proto-persona for a kick-off workshop
  • Listing every component state before handoff
  • Laying out a journey map for a workshop

UI designer

Owns the visual layer: type, color, spacing, grids.

  • Making a 50 to 950 color ramp from a brand color
  • Setting fluid type sizes with clamp() for the developers
  • Working out column widths and gutters for each breakpoint
  • Setting the text column width for 45 to 75 characters per line
  • Snapping line heights to a 4px grid
  • Picking icon sizes and stroke weights
  • Converting px to rem for a hand-off table

UX researcher

Plans studies, runs sessions, turns notes into findings.

  • Sizing a survey and reporting its margin of error
  • Scoring SUS and placing it against the 68 average
  • Checking survey questions for leading or double-barreled wording
  • Writing an interview guide timed to a 45-minute session
  • Reporting task completion rates with honest error bars
  • Picking labels for a Likert scale
  • Grouping interview notes for synthesis
  • Setting a fair participant incentive
  • Turning a vague ask into research questions

UX writer

Writes the words in the interface: labels, errors, empty states, help.

  • Checking button labels and toasts against length limits
  • Writing an error message that says what to do next
  • Estimating how much a string grows in translation
  • Checking help text for reading level
  • Deciding what alt text an image needs

Design system lead

Keeps the shared components, tokens and rules in shape.

  • Exporting a spacing scale as tokens
  • Naming tokens in a tiered pattern and finding the odd ones out
  • Producing a type scale as CSS variables and JSON
  • Checking the whole palette for contrast pairs
  • Making color ramps with contrast labels for each step

Accessibility specialist

Makes sure the product works for everyone, and can prove it.

  • Checking every text and background pair against WCAG
  • Testing focus order and keyboard traps with a checklist
  • Checking target sizes against WCAG 2.2
  • Advising on alt text for charts and screenshots
  • Logging issues with severity for a review

Scrum master or delivery lead

Runs the team's rhythm and protects its flow.

  • Working out sprint capacity with days off and meetings
  • Forecasting a release date from past velocity
  • Setting a WIP limit and seeing the effect on cycle time
  • Pulling cycle-time percentiles from a Jira export
  • Picking a retro format for a tired team
  • Drawing a burn-down for a team without tooling
  • Turning three-point guesses into a range

Product analyst

  • Building retention curves from a cohort table
  • Checking a finished A/B test for significance and sample-ratio problems
  • Sizing an A/B test before it starts
  • Writing a metric definition everyone reads the same way
  • Computing DAU/MAU stickiness
  • Computing cycle-time percentiles for the delivery review

Growth or pricing lead

  • Computing LTV, CAC and payback for a channel review
  • Finding the biggest drop in a funnel
  • Planning test duration from daily traffic
  • Reading a Van Westendorp price survey
  • Sketching good, better, best tiers
  • Modelling free-to-paid conversion for a revenue target
  • Checking what volume a discount needs to break even

Product marketer

Positions the product and launches it.

  • Filling a value proposition canvas
  • Computing NPS and whether a change is real
