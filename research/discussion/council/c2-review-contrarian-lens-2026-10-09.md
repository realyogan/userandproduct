**1. Strongest: B.** It is the only response a person could act on Monday. It prices the work (about 6 hours, 9 test runs), names the exact delivery on plain WordPress (.txt download, copy button, a "No skills? Paste this" block), refuses to promise Word output, and sets a stop rule. It also ties the skill to the PRD article that is already planned. Its one flaw is the three host-specific install lines, which C says would lose most designers and PMs. D's "reviewer, not generator" idea is the best single insight in the set, and B should take it.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It ignores the binding constraint the first council named, one author's time, and so recommits the "81 articles" mistake as a chained library with a paid team tier. It never deals with D and E's point that a blank ChatGPT box is the real competitor. It also assumes a GitHub repo will earn stars and backlinks for an unknown author. Hope is doing the work of evidence there.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **The numbers don't agree.** A says "prd template" is 2,900 searches at KD 6. B, D and E say 18,100 at KD 9 (B also cites KD 9). Every recommendation hangs on this one page, and nobody checked the figure before building on it.
- **The week-6 stop rules measure noise.** A new domain with no backlinks will have almost no organic traffic at week 6. "Under 50 copies" (B) or "skill against template" (D) tests LinkedIn reach, not the product.
- **Liability and data handling.** C only touches this. Nobody proposed a disclaimer, a license (CC BY with attribution, which would also answer E's scraping worry), or guidance on keeping confidential company data out of the assistant.
