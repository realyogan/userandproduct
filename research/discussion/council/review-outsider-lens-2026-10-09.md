# Peer review, Outsider lens

**1. Strongest: E.** It is the only response that does all three things: it puts the stated goal (authority first) next to the data, shows the two don't match (67% "what is X", AI Overviews everywhere, owner_fit left blank), and then gives a workable fix. Use the data to title and format pieces, not to pick them. Launch with 3 to 4 categories the owner can actually fill. C makes the same diagnosis just as sharply, but it ends on questions where E gives a plan. B is the most honest about how the site looks to a visitor, but it doesn't change what gets written.

**2. Biggest blind spot: D.** D is the most exciting of the five, but it ignores the constraint every other response raises: one author. On top of 81 articles it adds about 8 assets, a calculator, an email gate, a yearly research report, a Frameworks library, book lists from month one and a course. C and A show that even the bare plan doesn't fit one person's week. D also never asks whether gated downloads conflict with the authority goal, or whether readers would trust them.

**3. What all five missed:** nobody suggests asking real people before committing.
- Every argument is built from search data or from guesses about readers. No one says to show the category names, a "start here" page and two sample articles to 5 to 10 actual PMs and designers.
- No one asks what the owner already has: a network, talks, past writing, a LinkedIn audience. That decides whether authority-first is fast or slow.
- No one sets success or stop criteria, such as "by week 12, X readers or Y subscribers, or we change course." Without a checkpoint, six months of writing has no exit.
