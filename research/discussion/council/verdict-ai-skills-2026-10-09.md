# Council verdict 2, 9 Oct 2026: "AI skills" as a Templates product

The question was whether userandproduct.com should offer, inside Templates, a library of "AI skills": plain text instruction files a reader copies into ChatGPT or Claude so the assistant interviews them and then writes a finished BRD, PRD, user story set, roadmap and so on. If yes, how should it be built, positioned and launched?

Short answer: build one, not a library. It goes on the PRD template page as a third format beside the blank template and the worked example. Readers never see the word "skill". Before it ships, it has to beat a plain "interview me and write a PRD" prompt in a blind test. If it doesn't, it doesn't ship. The thing being sold is the owner's PRD method. The file is just one more way of handing it over.

**A correction first.** Three advisors (Executor, First Principles, Contrarian) quoted "prd template" at 18,100 searches a month. The first council's verdict quoted the same figure. Three of the five peer reviews caught that it clashed with the Expansionist's 2,900, and the research store settles it: "prd template" is **2,900 searches a month in the US at difficulty 6**, and "product requirements document template" is **2,900 at difficulty 11**. Once word-order duplicates are removed, the whole PRD-template phrase group comes to **about 4,500 a month**. The 18,100 belongs to a broader PRD head term, not the template query. The conclusion holds: the PRD page is still the first template piece because its page one is weak and the difficulty is low. The prize is smaller, though, which is another reason to keep this cheap. The first verdict's PRD line should be corrected to these numbers.

## Where the Council Agrees

**1. The value is the method, not the file.** All five land here in their own words. The First Principles Thinker says it most clearly: a PM searching for a PRD template doesn't know which questions to answer, in what order, or what a good answer looks like. The Word doc, the blank template, the article and the instruction file are four ways of packaging one method. The Expansionist calls the file "15 years of judgment written down". The Executor says "the owner's method is the product, and the assistant only delivers it". The Outsider would choose it over a free listicle prompt only because "this follows the structure I used for 15 years".

**2. The real competitor is a blank chat box.** The Contrarian and the First Principles Thinker both point out that any PM can type "interview me, then write a BRD" into ChatGPT in ten seconds and get most of the way there. A file without a real method in it is worth exactly that, and it would have the owner's name on it.

**3. Search demand is for the document, not the generator.** The research has no matches for "prd generator", "prompt for product managers" or "brd template with AI". Template intent, on the other hand, is proven: 33% of PRD searches, 34% of roadmap searches and 41% of user-story searches ask for a template or example. Every advisor, the Expansionist included, says the skill should ride on a template page and not stand alone.

**4. No separate section and no "full list" now.** Four of five say so outright, and every peer review faulted the Expansionist's three-skill launch, chain and public library for ignoring the one-author limit. The Executor priced a full list at about 15 skills times 6 hours, roughly 90 hours or 11 weeks of the two-pieces-a-week budget, spent before anyone has shown they want it. The Contrarian called it "the 81-article mistake again, the day after it was cut."

**5. Never promise Word or PDF.** The Executor, the First Principles Thinker, the Contrarian and the Outsider all agree. File output differs between assistants and between modes of the same assistant. The first time a reader hits a case where it doesn't work, they blame the site.

**6. No email gate.** This is consistent with the first verdict.

## Where the Council Clashes

**Signature format or small add-on.** The Expansionist sees a signature format: every template page as "template + skill + worked example", a linked chain from discovery plan to roadmap, a public library for backlinks, a hub for the "ai for product managers" terms (3,600 searches, difficulty 9) and a path to a paid team tier. Everyone else sees one add-on to one page. **The minority loses on timing, not on direction.** None of the Expansionist's upside has a cost estimate, and all of it assumes the method beats a plain prompt, which nobody has shown yet. The upside stays on the table as a path that unlocks only if the first one works (see below).

**Generator or reviewer.** The owner asked for an interview that writes the document. The First Principles Thinker argues that generating a PRD is a commodity and judging one is scarce: "paste your draft and I'll check it against these 12 tests." Four of five peer reviews called the reviewer the best single idea in the set. **Ship both, from one method, in one block.** The interview serves the template searcher who starts from nothing. The reviewer serves the PM who already has a draft and shows the owner's judgment most clearly. Both run on the same questions and the same quality bar, so the reviewer costs very little extra.

**Copyable: a flaw or the way it spreads.** The Contrarian says a text file gets scraped into public lists without the owner's name, so the moat is given away. The Expansionist says scraping is distribution. **The Expansionist is right once the credit travels inside the file** (an Executor-lens review point) and the license requires attribution (a Contrarian-lens review point). With both in place, every copy carries the owner's name and URL.

**What to call it.** The Executor would label it "the PRD interview I run, as an AI skill". The Outsider says "skill", "Markdown", "agent", "install" and "project instruction" lose most designers and half of PMs before the second sentence. **Side with the Outsider.** The audience is working PMs, and the words that work for them are "interview", "draft", "you review it", "works in ChatGPT and Claude" and "about 10 minutes".

**One setup path or several.** The Executor wants three install lines, one per assistant. The Outsider says more than one setup path is a reason to leave. **One path on the page:** a copy button and a "paste this into ChatGPT or Claude" line. A single small download link sits below it for people whose tools load instruction files. Nothing else.

**Can it be measured?** The Contrarian says copied text leaves no trace. The Executor's answer is to record an analytics event on copy and on download. Both are partly right. Counts show reach and say nothing about quality, so the measurement below adds outputs that readers send back.

## Blind Spots the Council Caught

These came out only in peer review:

- **The search figure was wrong, and nobody checked it.** Three advisors built on 18,100. The reviews caught the clash with 2,900, and the store confirms 2,900 at difficulty 6, with about 4,500 across the phrase group.
- **Nobody tested whether the method beats a plain prompt.** Every advisor assumed it does. If the owner's question set doesn't clearly beat "interview me and write a PRD" on the same brief, the file is worthless whatever its format or launch plan. This is the most important point the reviews raised.
- **The credit can travel inside the file and inside the output.** A header line in the file gives the owner's name, the method's name, the page URL, a version and a "last tested" date. One deletable line at the foot of the finished PRD names the method and links back. Every copy and every document passed around a company then works as distribution.
- **License, liability and confidential data.** No advisor proposed a license, a disclaimer or guidance on what to keep out of the assistant. Many PMs work under employer rules that allow only one approved assistant, or none.
- **The company's own template.** Most PMs have to use their organization's PRD format. A file that forces the owner's structure won't fit. It should accept "here is our template" and still apply the owner's questions and quality bar.
- **Week-6 numbers measure LinkedIn, not the product.** A new domain with no backlinks has almost no search traffic at week 6. Copy counts at that point measure reach.
- **Counts don't show quality.** Ask real users to send back what it produced.
- **The method isn't written down yet.** The file depends on the PRD article, so the article comes first.
- **Upkeep when the assistants change.** The file needs a pinned "last tested" date and a re-test every quarter.
- **A PRD is a negotiation.** The output should end with the open questions to take to engineering and stakeholders, not pretend to be final.
- **The owner can use it first.** Running it on a real brief produces the worked example the template page needs anyway.

## The Recommendation

**Build it at all? Yes, one, and only after it passes a blind test.** The cost is a few hours on top of an article the first verdict already puts first. If it fails, those hours still buy a sharper PRD article and a worked example.

**One or several? One: PRD.** It is the first template piece, its page one is weak, and the owner's own BRD is a ready-made worked example. User stories and acceptance criteria come next, and only if PRD passes its checks.

**Reviewer, interview, or both? Both, in one block, from one method.**
- **Interview:** it asks the owner's 10 to 12 questions one at a time, pushes back on vague answers, accepts the reader's company template if they paste one, and then drafts the PRD.
- **Check my draft:** the reader pastes an existing PRD and gets it scored against the same tests, with specific fixes.
- **Rules built into the file, both modes:**
  - It marks gaps as "[TBD: you didn't say]" and never fills them in.
  - It never invents metrics, benchmarks, dates or customer facts.
  - Every requirement gets an acceptance line.
  - It ends with "Questions to take to engineering and stakeholders".

**Name and wording (the Outsider's vocabulary).**
- On the page: **"PRD interview"**, with "Check my draft" as its second mode.
- Headline line: *"Paste this into ChatGPT or Claude. It interviews you for about 10 minutes, then drafts your PRD. You review it."*
- Never shown to readers: "skill", "AI skill", "prompt", "prompt library", "Markdown", "agent", "install", or any model name. "Skill" can stay as the internal word.
- Name the method itself, for example "the User and the Product PRD method" (the owner picks the final name). A named method is what AI answers quote.

**Where it lives.** On the PRD template page, at its flat URL, as the third format next to the blank template and the worked example. The worked example shows the full interview: the questions, the answers and the finished PRD. That before-and-after is the whole sale.
- One copy button (a few lines of plain JavaScript) with a "Copied" state.
- One small "download as a text file" link, for tools that load instruction files. A .txt file, because the site's uploader blocks .md.
- No AI section, no Templates sub-category and no separate URL. The Templates index lists the PRD template, which mentions the interview.

**What it must never promise:**
- a Word or PDF file (the output is a draft that pastes into Word or Google Docs);
- a finished or "ready to send" document;
- accuracy about the reader's business;
- that it is safe for confidential data;
- that it replaces the review with engineering.

The page says plainly: *"It drafts. You decide. Anything you didn't tell it is marked TBD, not made up."*

**How it carries the owner's credit.**
- A header in the file: method name, "by [owner name]", the page URL, version (v1.0) and "last tested [month year]".
- A deletable footer line in every output: *"Structured with the [method name], userandproduct.com/[prd-page]"*.
- The page states the owner's experience in one sentence ("the structure I've used for 15 years").

**License and confidential data.**
- License the file under **CC BY 4.0**: anyone may copy, adapt and share it, including commercially, as long as they credit the owner and link back. Scraping becomes credited distribution.
- A short disclaimer: provided as is; the reader is responsible for what they send.
- A plain "Before you paste" box:
  - Use only the assistant your employer approves. If it allows none, use the blank template instead.
  - Swap customer names, revenue figures, unreleased product names and personal data for placeholders such as [Customer A] and [$X].
  - Turn off chat history or training where your assistant allows it.

**Owner hours: a cap of 6 extra hours, then it counts as a weekly piece.**

| Task | Hours | Who |
|---|---|---|
| Writing the 10 to 12 questions and the quality tests | 0 extra | Already part of the PRD article |
| Turning the questions into the file and the page block | about 1 hour of owner review | A helper drafts it |
| Running the blind test with 5 PMs | about 2 to 3 hours | Owner |
| 6 runs across 3 briefs and both assistants, plus the worked example | about 1.5 hours | Owner reviews the runs |
| Page copy | about 0.5 hours | Owner |
| Quarterly re-test once live | about 1 hour each quarter | Owner |

If the extra work goes past 6 hours, the PRD interview takes one of the week's two slots and something else moves back. It never pushes a launch article out.

**The test that proves the method beats a plain prompt.** Run it before the interview goes live. It blocks only the interview, never the article or the site.
1. Write 3 test briefs: a good one, a vague one and a contradictory one.
2. Run each brief twice in ChatGPT and in Claude:
   - with the PRD interview;
   - with the plain prompt "Interview me, then write a PRD."
3. Remove all labels and give the pairs to **5 working PMs**. Each answers, for each pair:
   - which one would you send to your director?
   - what in it is made up?
4. **Pass:** the PRD interview is preferred in at least 4 of 5 judges' overall picks, and it invents nothing on the vague and contradictory briefs while the plain prompt does.
5. **Fail:** the article and the blank template ship without the interview. The "Check my draft" mode can be tested on its own once. If that fails too, the idea is closed.

**Measurement and stop rule, aligned with the first council's checkpoints.**

| Checkpoint | What to check | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Day 14 | Nothing about the PRD interview | The live site comes first, as the first verdict says. The interview must not slow it down. |
| Week 3 | The blind test is done | Ship the interview with the PRD article if it passed. If not, the article ships alone. |
| Week 6, alongside the reader test the first verdict already plans | Copy and download events, counted from every source and mostly from LinkedIn; outputs from **5 PMs who used it on real work and sent back the result** | Go: at least 3 of the 5 would use it again unprompted, **and** either 50 or more copies and downloads or 3 or more unprompted peer mentions. Anything less: no second one, and the PRD interview stays as it is. |
| Month 3 | Whether the next two are working | If week 6 passed, add the user story interview, then acceptance criteria. Each counts as a weekly piece. Cap: 3 in total. |
| Month 6 | On the PRD page, compare how often the interview is copied with how often the template is downloaded | If the interview is copied less than the template is downloaded, freeze the set at what exists. |

Copy counts never decide alone. The returned outputs and the "would use again" answer carry the weight.

**The long-term path, only if the first test passes.**
- **Month 3:** up to 3 interviews (PRD, user stories, acceptance criteria). Each sits on its own template page and is re-tested every quarter.
- **Month 4 to 5, once three exist:**
  - Publish them together in a public, versioned repository under the same CC BY license, with a changelog. This is the Expansionist's backlink bet, tried cheaply on an asset that is already finished.
  - Write one signature article, "How I use ChatGPT and Claude to write product documents", aimed at the "ai for product managers" and "ai for product management" searches (3,600 each, difficulty 9). It links the interviews. It is not a listicle.
- **After 5 interviews pass the bar:** link them in a chain (discovery plan, PRD, user stories) so each one reads the output of the one before.
- **Month 6 review, only if search and authority signals are moving:** decide on a paid team version (one shared method for a whole team) or a course built on the questions, where each question becomes a lesson.
- **Throughout:** never call any of it a "prompt library", and never open an AI section.

## The One Thing to Do First

This week, while drafting the PRD article, the owner writes down the 10 to 12 questions they actually ask before writing a PRD, and the tests they use to judge one, in one sitting of about 45 minutes. That list is the method. It sharpens the article, feeds the worked example, and becomes the PRD interview and the "Check my draft" mode. Then run the blind test against the plain prompt. Nothing else about this idea gets built until that test is passed.
