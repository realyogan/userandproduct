**1. Strongest: B.** It's the only response I could act on by Monday. It sets one skill, one page and an hour count (about 6). It tests three kinds of brief on three hosts, adds a stop rule with a number (50 copies plus downloads by week 6) and says never to promise Word. D gets to the same place and adds the best single idea, a reviewer that checks a PRD I've already drafted, but it's less concrete. **2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It's written for people who already know what GitHub repos, coding-agent plugins and chained skills are. It never asks whether a working designer or PM would understand "AI skill" at all, and C shows they wouldn't. It also skips capacity: three tested skills plus a repo plus a chain, a day after the plan was cut to 12 pieces. And it ignores the risk E and C raise, that the AI makes up content in a BRD that carries the owner's name.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Workplace AI rules.** Many PMs can't paste company plans into consumer ChatGPT. Their employer allows only Copilot or Gemini, or no AI at all. Nobody tested those tools or wrote guidance for them, and C only touches on it.
- **The company's own template.** Most PMs have to use their organization's PRD format. A skill that imposes the owner's structure won't fit. It should accept "here's our template" and still apply the owner's questions and quality bar.
- **A PRD is a negotiation, not just a document.** None of the five say how the output gets reviewed with engineers and stakeholders.
