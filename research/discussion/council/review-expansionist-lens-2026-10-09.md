# Peer review, Expansionist lens

**1. Strongest: D.** It is the only response that reads the data as an opportunity and not just a risk. Every head term has an AI Overview, and D's answer is to build the things an Overview can't hand over: calculators, filled-in templates (user stories 41%, PRDs 33%) and book lists. It also links those assets to email signups, which is a BRD objective the plan dropped, and to a first course built from the Wave 1 material. Its best idea is publishing the research itself as a yearly "what UX and product people search for" report, which earns links and builds authority from day one. Its weak spot is that it skips the owner's limited writing time, and E's "which pieces can only this owner write" filter fills that gap.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** A is right that the plan needs to ship, but it speeds up the wrong product. It keeps the commodity tree as it is and cuts Links, Books and Tools, which are the assets AI can't absorb and the quickest route to the owner's name and affiliate income. Shipping faster doesn't help if the pages are interchangeable with Atlassian's.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Being cited by AI.** Everyone treated AI Overviews only as a threat. Pages built around original frameworks, named methods and the owner's own data are the ones Overviews and chat assistants cite, and a citation builds authority even without a click.
- **A short glossary layer.** The 67% "what is X" demand doesn't need 81 full articles. Short, linked definition pages would capture it cheaply and send readers to the opinion pieces. B used "glossary" as a criticism, but it is a format worth using.
- **Interviews and guest practitioners.** They lift the one-author limit and widen the owner's network, which is authority in both directions.
- **Consulting, speaking and advisory leads.** This is the fastest return on authority, and no response mentioned it.
