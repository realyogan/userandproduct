# Peer review, Executor lens

**1. Strongest: A.** It is the only response that answers "what happens Monday." It names a first article (PRD template and examples, KD 9, which also gives Templates a launch item for free). It redoes the cadence maths at two pieces a week. It closes the four open decisions with defaults and makes the one irreversible choice safe with flat /article-slug/ permalinks. It also sets a clear test: no article live within two weeks means it is still planning. Its weakness is that it picks topics by KD and only gives owner_fit ten minutes. C and E are right that this input matters more than that.

**2. Biggest blind spot: D.** It answers "too much work for one author" by adding more. It proposes 81 articles plus about 8 assets, an email gate, a yearly research report, a Frameworks library, affiliate books from month one and a course. A three-mode calculator is software that needs testing, and an email gate means a mail provider, consent handling and a privacy page. D never asks who builds all this or when. B has a similar gap from the reader's side. Its renamed categories and "start here" paths are good, but it gives no cost and no order.

**3. What all five missed: there is no live site, no feedback loop and no test of the cadence.** Everything runs on local XAMPP. Nobody mentions buying hosting, deploying, getting indexed or setting up Search Console, so "publish now" (A) has nowhere to go. Nobody sets kill or pivot criteria either, such as impressions by week 8 or LinkedIn reactions per piece. Nobody suggests a timed pilot: write three real pieces this week, measure the hours, then size the waves from that number instead of an assumed three a week. The fastest path is to ship a public site with 3 to 5 pieces, watch real signals for 6 to 8 weeks, and then commit to the tree.
