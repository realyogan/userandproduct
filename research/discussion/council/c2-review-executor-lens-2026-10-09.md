# Council 2 peer review, Executor lens

**1. Strongest: B.** It's the only response you could act on Monday morning. It gives a file spec (four blocks, the same frontmatter format as llm-council) and a test matrix (3 briefs × 3 hosts = 9 runs, about 3 hours). It costs the work at about 6 hours, or 3 to 4 if bundled with the PRD article. It works around a real WordPress problem: WordPress blocks `.md` uploads, so the file ships as `.txt` with a copy button. It also tracks copies and downloads as a GA4 event and sets a stop rule (under 50 copies plus downloads at week 6 means no second skill). D reaches the same shape, and its "ship a reviewer" idea is the best new idea in the set. But D's "about an hour" leaves out testing, so it's wrong.

**2. Biggest blind spot: A.** It recommends launching three skills plus a public GitHub repo, a chain of linked skills and a paid tier, with no hours estimate. That ignores the one-author constraint the first council named. A also quotes "prd template" at 2,900 searches (KD 6), while B, D and E use 18,100 (KD 9), and it never notices the clash. And it doesn't answer E's point that a public repo makes the work easy to scrape without the owner's name.

**3. What all five missed:**
- **Put the credit inside the file.** A header line with the owner's name, the canonical URL and a version number travels with every copy and scrape. Scraping then becomes free distribution instead of theft (this answers E). Nobody proposed it.
- **The method isn't written down yet.** Every plan depends on the PRD article that captures it, and nobody blocks the skill on that piece being drafted first.
- **Settle the 2,900 vs 18,100 figure from the cache** before it shapes priorities.
- **Upkeep when models change.** A pinned "last tested" date and a re-test once a quarter would cover it. Nobody budgeted for either.
- **Copy counts don't show quality.** Ask a few readers to send back their outputs at week 6, alongside the counts.
