# The question for the council

The owner of userandproduct.com (a solo author with 15 years in product design and product management, building a Smashing-Magazine-style authority site at the crossing of UX, product management and business) has a proposal for a "newsroom" of AI agents that would plan, write, illustrate, check, publish, distribute and monitor the site's articles. The owner asks the council: **what are the gaps in this system, and how should it be improved?** The owner will review the council's verdict tomorrow before deciding whether and how to build it.

## The proposal in short

- Five desks of agents around one backlog: Planning (an explorer that keeps a backlog of article ideas full from six sources: the paid keyword store, weekly competitor sitemap diffs, cached "people also ask" questions, reading the top five live pages per idea, our own coverage map, the owner's story bank), Making (researcher, writer, illustrator, thumbnail maker, checker, SEO reviewer, editor), Publishing (publisher to WordPress plus live-page verification), Distribution (LinkedIn writer; a reel maker later), Performance (a campaign manager that reads Search Console, analytics, page speed, the keyword store and the sitemap diffs weekly; scores the site, each category and each article against an "expectation" set at approval; writes nudges and refresh jobs with evidence; never changes the site itself; also keeps a review date on every page and sends old pages back through the making desk).
- The main session is the managing editor and routes every hand-off; agents do not call each other.
- The gate for an idea is one sentence: "the reader learns X, which the top five pages do not teach." Search volume ranks survivors, never admits them.
- Backlog water marks: keep 20 approved ideas, refill below 12.
- The owner's routine: on Sunday, approve or kill ideas (10 min), say "write six", give a story where one is flagged, then read the editor's one-page summary per draft and approve, then publish or schedule. Two stop signs per article (after the brief, optional; after the editor). Nothing publishes without the owner.
- Each article lives in one folder with brief, draft, figures, thumbnail, checks, SEO review, final.
- Corrections by the owner go into a feedback file per desk that the agent reads next time.
- Honest limits stated in the proposal: WordPress is not installed yet, so publishing cannot run; posting to LinkedIn and Instagram by API needs app approval, so the first version produces packs the owner posts by hand; voiceover needs a speech service.
- Twelve agents in the end: ten in the first build, a reel maker later, the campaign manager after launch. Five rulebooks (skills) still to write: illustrations, article SEO review, WordPress publishing, LinkedIn, performance review. Rulebooks that exist: writing style (an "experienced teacher" voice, guide with guardrails, specifics from the owner's experience), thumbnails (eleven styles with a generator), the SEO research store.
- Build order: backlog and explorer; illustration and SEO rulebooks; the making desk run once on the first article with a real story; WordPress install and publisher; LinkedIn pack; reels; campaign manager after launch.

## Context the advisors should know

- Goal order, set by the owner: (1) authority in both directions, the owner's experience backs the site and the site backs the owner; (2) organic search traffic; later books, a links directory, tools, templates, courses.
- The site does not exist yet. Nothing is live, no hosting, no Search Console, no backlinks. All work so far is research (a $3.55 keyword and SERP pass), a topic tree of about 210 possible articles in 13 categories, a logo, page mockups and the two rulebooks above.
- An earlier council (same day) reviewed the launch plan and concluded: the research is sound; shrink the launch to about 12 pieces in six weeks; two pieces a week is the realistic cadence for one author, a third is a bonus; put the owner's own stories at the centre because a tidy explainer from this author reads like anyone's unless first-hand specifics carry it; LinkedIn is the main channel; set checkpoints and a stop rule; a new domain with no links will not rank even for easy terms; test the cadence on three real pieces before planning on it; AI answer engines quote pages with original frameworks and the owner's own data.
- The owner's own words on the gate: the primary objective is value, "people should learn something and think: I haven't seen this approach, this is useful for me."
- The owner has said the Sunday number could be "five or six articles" a week, written by the agents from the backlog; the owner supplies stories and approvals.
- Constraints: dependency-light, everything free or cheap, a hard cap on paid keyword spend, no AI attribution anywhere a reader can see, US English.

## What is at stake

If the system is built as proposed and the gaps are not caught, the owner spends several sessions building a machine that either produces articles that do not build authority (generic, machine-flavoured, or without the owner's experience in them), or demands more of the owner's time than the Sunday hour promises, or publishes faster than a new site can earn trust, or optimises for the wrong signal. The owner wants the system to be genuinely good, not just to exist.
