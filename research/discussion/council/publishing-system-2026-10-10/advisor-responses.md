# Advisor responses (round 1, independent)

Each advisor read question.md and proposal-5-extract.txt and answered alone.

## The Contrarian

**The fatal flaw: nobody in this system supplies the X.** Every card promises "the reader learns X, which the top five pages do not teach." Under proposal 5 the owner gives "direction, not stories," "many cards will have none," and "the content is written by the agents." So where does X come from? The explorer reads the top five pages, which by definition lack it. From sources that lack X, an agent can only remix or invent. The sample note gives it away: "Keep the example from a real product team." Which team? The hard rule bans only *first-person* claims. It does not ban invented third-person case studies or made-up metrics. The checker checks "against the sources," and an invented example has no source to fail against. Authority is goal one, and this design produces fifty fluent pieces of synthesis.
*Change:* add a required "where X comes from" field on every card: the owner's note, a named source, or "derived" (the reasoning written out). A card whose X is "derived" with no note from the owner cannot be approved. Extend the hard rule to every invented example, team, number or quote, and have the checker list each one with its origin.

**The gate is graded by the people who set it.** The explorer writes the learning sentence, and the editor asks only "does it deliver the learning the card promised?" Nobody checks the promise itself.
*Change:* the checker, working cold, quotes any of the top five pages that already teaches X. One hit sends the card back.

**The review-time math leaves out the second read.** "The draft comes back to you" after your fixes, and that re-read is not in the 30 to 40 minutes. Neither is writing ten notes at each meeting. And "the same mistake does not come back" is a hope: the feedback folder grows until its notes contradict each other.
*Change:* time the first window from start to finish, re-reads included, and set the stock size from that number, not from fifty. Each window, the editor folds the feedback into the writing-style rulebook and empties the folder.

**The build order puts the expensive part before the risky part.** Steps 1 and 2 build the app, the explorer, coverage mode, /go and the window runner before step 3 shows whether a draft is worth reading. If drafts come back thin, all of that is wasted.
*Change:* do step 3 first, with three cards written by hand in a JSON file and the drafts read as plain preview pages. Then build the app around what that window needed.

**The expectation line is precision with nothing behind it.** "Top 10 within 90 days, and 50 clicks a month by day 120," on a domain with no links. The campaign manager then scores pages against a guess.
*Change:* until performance mode, expectations name leading signals only (indexed, impressions on the target phrase, links earned). Rank targets come later, from the site's own data.

## The First Principles Thinker

Strip it down. Goal one is authority: the owner's fifteen years back the site, and the site backs the owner. So the real question is not "how do we produce articles well?" It is "how does the owner's judgment get onto the page at the lowest cost per insight?" Proposal 5 answers the first question superbly and barely touches the second.

Follow the knowledge, not the paper. "The content is written by the agents." The researcher reads "the top five pages." The writer writes from "the brief and your note," and the note is about form: "Lead with the one-page rule. Drop the history of templates." A story is "an extra, never a requirement." Every input except the owner is public. Remixing public sources yields a polished summary of what is known. The learning sentence promises "X, which the top five pages do not teach," yet nothing requires X to come from anywhere. Without the owner, X is an agent's guess at novelty.

The proposal's own example card gives it away: "Keep the example from a real product team, not a made-up one." Which team? Nothing on the belt can get that except the owner. The rule against first-person claims the owner did not give is right, but it only stops faked expertise.

Door 3, the brief, already has the right shape: "Your rules form the spine of the piece... The research fills in the why and the examples." That is the product. Here it is a side door, when it should be the main road.

The ramp gate points backwards. "Your review time per piece has come down" is the sign of a process that has gelled. But the owner's time is the only part of this system that creates authority. Treating less of it as progress trades goal one for goal two.

The launch has the same flaw. The owner's LinkedIn network judges those fifty pieces first, and they know his work best. Competent summaries read as "he outsourced it": the opposite of authority.

What I would change:

1. **Replace the note with a seed on every card.** Before each meeting the explorer drafts two or three questions from practice, aimed where the top pages fall short ("They hand out 12 sections. Which five survive on a real team, and why?"). The owner answers by voice in two or three minutes. The answers are the spine, stated as rules and judgments, with no first person needed.
2. **Trace the learning sentence.** Every X points to a seed answer or a primary source, shown on the card.
3. **Label cards "seeded" or "synthesis."** Cap synthesis at about a quarter of output, and never use it for the launch's flagship pieces.
4. **Give the editor a second question:** "Is there a judgment here no top-five page could have written?"
5. **Gate the ramp on draft quality,** and track seed minutes per piece as investment, not cost.
6. **Pick the ten launch pieces the network will open first,** and seed those hardest.

## The Expansionist

The most valuable thing in this design is not the ten agents. It is two small boxes: "your note" and the brief's seed, "your rules are saved word for word." Everything else can be bought or copied. Fifteen years of rules cannot. The proposal treats them as inputs one article uses up. They should build up into the site's lasting asset.

**1. Turn the owner's rules into a canon.** Door 3 is the strongest door, and right now it is the smallest. Every rule the owner gives, in a brief, a note or a correction, should go into one principles file (research/editorial/principles.md). Each rule gets an id, the owner's exact words, the "from experience" flag, and the pieces that use it. Within a year that is a named, citable body of work, which is the authority goal made concrete. A principles page earns links no single article can. Ask for two or three rules at every /go, not only when a brief comes up.

**2. Give every card an asset field.** The house rule says the reader should "feel they did something on every screen," and the BRD lists templates and calculators for later. Add "Takeaway asset" next to "Format": a template, checklist, worksheet or small calculator that ships with the piece. Fifty launch articles then also means thirty-odd templates. The templates section fills itself, people link to the assets, and the product line the BRD defers starts now.

**3. Publish the explorer's log.** Every rival page is logged as "gap, take or nothing," along with what the top five pages miss. That is original data about the whole field. Once a quarter it becomes a "What the field didn't teach" report. For a new domain, original research is the surest way to earn links, and the explorer already produces this data as a by-product.

**4. Give takes more weight.** "Two to four in fifty" is too cautious for the one kind of idea "measured on authority." Takes are what people share. Schedule three or four strong takes for the announcement week, then one or two in every block.

**5. Make the newsletter a pipeline, not a sign-up box.** The report and the approved pieces already exist as data. A weekly letter from the owner (one rule, one link, one asset) falls out of each window without a new agent. An owned audience gives the authority loop that a separate LinkedIn page cannot.

**6. Let the book door feed the shelf.** Each mined book drafts its own shelf entry, which waits for the owner's one-paragraph take. Affiliate income grows out of the editorial work.

**7. Treat each cluster as a course outline.** Ask the explorer to put each category's seven or eight pieces in learning order. Series pages read better, and each cluster is a course module ready for later.

Re-weighting: build the principles file and the asset field in step 1, together with the backlog data. Each costs one schema change and makes every later piece worth more.

## The Outsider

I read this as a stranger, and then as a reader of the finished site. The machine is easy to follow. What it produces worries me.

**The byline and the text promise different things.** The author page "carries your fifteen years." The text is shaped by a note where "a sentence is often enough," and a story is "an extra, never a requirement." The hard rule, "no first-person claim you did not give," is honest, but look at what it does: where there is no story, the writer must leave the author out. So most pieces will be careful research syntheses with an expert's name on top. "Better than the top five pages" is a researcher's standard, not a practitioner's. Nothing in the proposal shows me where a reader sees the fifteen years on the page itself.

**The gate is written by the party that benefits from it.** The explorer reads the top five pages, then writes the sentence saying it beats them. The editor then asks whether the draft "delivers the learning the card promised." The same system writes the promise and grades it. No outside reader ever checks whether a newcomer actually learns anything.

**The source check goes in a circle.** The checker verifies claims "against the sources," and those are the sources the researcher picked. The example note asks for "the example from a real product team, not a made-up one." Who finds that team, and how does the checker know it is real?

**The ramp rewards skimming.** One condition for speeding up is "your review time per piece has come down." Reading faster and reading worse look the same on that measure. Thirty hours for fifty pieces is reading time. It leaves nothing for rewriting.

**Insider shorthand a newcomer trips over:** "the tree," "mockup A," "tint," "Printables," "the store," "/go." The metaphors stack three deep. The proposal says "Four kinds of idea come out," lists five kinds on the card, and draws six spots in the circles. The "director" that "directs from day one" only counts things until there is data.

**What I would change, pipeline first:**
1. Give every card one required "only I know this" field: a number, a mistake, a decision, a real artifact. Make it one line, not a story. Without it, the card does not go to the window. That is the authority gate.
2. Add a cold reader before the owner reads. A fresh agent, given no brief, reads final.md and writes what it learned in one sentence. If that sentence does not match the card's learning sentence, the draft goes back.
3. Have the checker pull its own second source for every claim the piece depends on. Never let it accept a named example it cannot link to.
4. Replace "review time has come down" with "fixes per draft have come down." Measure quality, not speed.
5. Before the launch stock, show five outsiders three pieces with the byline removed. Ask one question: "Does this read like someone who has done the job?"
6. Add a short glossary and keep one metaphor.

## The Executor

"Each build step is a session or two." Step 1 isn't. It holds the backlog schema, a five-tab app (Board, Cards, Report, Timeline with snapshot replay, Preview with corrections marked on the paragraph), an explorer with eleven sources, coverage mode, /go and the window runner. That is five or six sessions, and the owner still has zero articles, because the making desk only arrives in step 3.

**Dependencies that bite:**

- **The app can't save.** "Your clicks, notes and corrections go back as data," but a plain HTML and vanilla JS page cannot write files. It needs a PHP save endpoint under XAMPP, plus paragraph IDs that stay stable through redrafts. Neither is mentioned.
- **Coverage mode has nothing to measure.** "Index coverage in Search Console... direct visits." There is no domain, hosting, Search Console or analytics. Before launch, coverage is "count published against the tree": a 30-line script, not an agent.
- **The theme isn't in the build order.** Step 4 says "WordPress locally, the publisher." Nothing builds the child theme, the structure plugin, Rank Math, category and series pages, start-here, the author page, the books shelf or the newsletter sign-up. Those are what make "no empty corners" true. It's a second project of four to six sessions, sitting unscheduled on the critical path.
- **The explorer isn't needed before launch.** The tree already holds about 210 scored ideas, so fifty launch cards can come from tree-simplified-v1.1. Mailbox, Reddit, the books door and rival two-pass reading can wait.
- **Hand-off volume.** Seven stations times ten pieces is seventy runs per window. The window runner must be a script that walks article folders by file state, not a chat conversation.
- **Owner-hours.** Fifty at 35 minutes is about 30 hours, or 1.5 to 2 hours a day over 15 to 20 days. Plan the stock at 30.

**Fastest credible path:**

1. **Monday.** Hand-write three cards as JSON from the tree. Run a three-station belt: research plus writing, figures plus thumbnail, then checking, SEO and editing. Render final.md in mockup A with a static script. The owner marks fixes in a feedback file and times each read. One session gives a real number.
2. **In parallel, from Tuesday.** Local WordPress, child theme, plugin, Rank Math, section pages, then the publisher with WP-CLI scheduled posts. Buy the domain and hosting now, because DNS and mailbox setup take days of waiting.
3. **A second window of six.** Add the Cards tab and the PHP save. Split stations only where the merged ones failed.
4. **The launch stock**, built in windows.
5. **After launch.** Explorer sources, the campaign manager's real modes, Timeline, takes, books, voice.

**Cut or defer:** Timeline snapshots, the kanban, the mailbox, the books door, takes before launch, the ten-agent split, and the campaign manager as an agent before Search Console exists. Make the making desk first and run WordPress alongside it, starting now. Grow the machine out of the first three articles, not the other way round.
