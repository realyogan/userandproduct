# Notes on a video the owner shared: Google's October 1 helpful-content changes and AI content

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qMUAU7E3dCA (Matt Kenyon). Shared by the owner on 10 October 2026 as a
transcript; these are our notes, not the transcript.

## What the video says

- On 1 October 2026 Google changed two pages: the helpful-content guide (added the "main content" idea and the four
  rater criteria: effort, originality, talent or skill, accuracy; named fake authors with AI headshots or false
  credentials as deception) and the generative-AI guidance (fact-check and review all AI-generated content before
  publishing, including titles, meta descriptions and alt text). We had already read both pages in full the same day;
  the rulebook carries them.
- Quality raters do not rank pages; their ratings train the ranking systems, so the rater criteria say what the systems
  learn to reward.
- Pattern claimed, citing Lily Ray: Google tends to publish guidance shortly before a major update (Panda 2011,
  Penguin 2012, Medic 2018, a core update in May 2026 after the spam-policy clarification for AI overviews). Four spam
  updates in 2026 so far. A Google engineer said scaled content is becoming a bigger problem than link spam. The
  speaker expects an update aimed at scaled AI content.
- Sites hit this year, per Lily Ray's article: scaled pages (hundreds of thousands in a short window, templated
  city or comparison pages, topics the site has no experience in); fake authors; self-promotional "best X" listicles
  by vendors; paid placements in other sites' best-of lists (effectively bought links). Several hit sites said human
  editors checked every draft and were demoted anyway: review alone is not originality.
- Signs of low-effort machine text the speaker looks for: hedging, no real opinion, over-structured prose, circling
  the point, "it's not X, it's Y", em dashes, general knowledge any model would produce ("meat proxy": a person
  passing a model's output to the reader unchanged).
- Positive examples: Crutchfield (own photos, own videos, custom diagrams, staff shown doing the work, working wiring
  tools), Regie.ai (studies from its own first-party data).
- The speaker's advice: start from something the model does not have (own data, a customer call, a test, a strong
  opinion against consensus) and let AI shape it; fact-check every number, quote and claim by hand; read every word
  and rewrite anything that sounds like a model; a real author with a real bio; say how AI was used; do not scale
  past your own expertise.

## Where our plan already covers it

Effort, originality, skill and accuracy are the frame of the search-policy rulebook (section 2); fake authors are
banned (section 5); metadata review is in the app preview and the checklist (section 7); question farms and templated
pages are banned (section 3); paid links and placements are banned (section 9); the cluster rule keeps the site inside
the owner's expertise; the pace ramp is gated on quality; the seed on every card is the "something the model does not
have".
