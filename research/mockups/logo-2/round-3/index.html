Round 3: the coin, the orbit, and fresh marks

Numbering changed on 10 Oct. The owner reviewed an earlier version of this board. Earlier numbers, then now: R3-05 Square coin (removed here, carried into round 4), R3-06 Bead in the track (now R3-05), R3-07 Broken ring (now R3-06), R3-08 Heavy arc (now R3-07), R3-09 Linked rings (now R3-08), R3-10 Square orbit (now R3-09), R3-11 Eclipse (now R3-10), R3-12 Reader (now R3-11), R3-13 Crossing (now R3-17), R3-14 Shelf (now R3-16); R3-15 Shield, R3-16 T-shaped and R3-17 Phi were dropped. R3-01 to R3-04 did not move. R3-12 to R3-15 and R3-18 are new. From now on boards are never renumbered after review: new marks go at the end and dropped marks stay, marked as dropped.

The brief

  1. Authority and trust first: a serious publication a professional would cite.
  2. Mass, one idea, a two-tone fold where forms overlap; nothing thinner than 8 at 512.
  3. App-icon feel: most marks led by a circle or rounded square, a few standing free; legible at 32 px.
  4. Every mark in monochrome: white on black, black on white, and one color with no fold.
  5. Blue by default (signal blue with a lighter fold); four marks in an alternative color.

The standard for cleanness: a solid disc with one sharp figure cut from it, no fuss. Letters are a minority here: two of eighteen marks are letter-based (R3-01, R3-02); group C is figurative.

Dropped after rounds 1 and 2

  • Rockets, all of them.
  • The folder bust ("some kind of stamp").
  • The switch ("doesn't make sense").
  • The bookmark (nice, not strong, seen before).
  • Also kept out: cursors, folders, keys, pens, library icons, and a profile head in a disc (the old exploration tried it).

The idea
The coin with a deep U slot cut from the top; the two pole caps are the lighter tone, so the coin reads as a magnet.
Why "oh nice"
A product that pulls people in, and the pull is drawn with nothing but one cut in a coin.
What it means
The coin is the product. The slot is cut so the coin pulls like a magnet. A good product draws people back, and the site is about how to make one.
Authority check
A full, heavy disc with one clean slot: it sits like a seal or a coin, not a toy. The magnet reading is quiet enough not to look like a novelty.
Construction
Disc 512. Slot 128 wide with a round foot at y 364. The caps above y 150 are the second tone; in monochrome an 18-unit gap separates them, so the poles survive in one color.
Nearest existing logos
  • Udemy (a heavy U as the mark)
  • the magnet icon in common icon sets
  • a slotted disc in the old exploration, round 5 (a thin slot ending in a hole)

The idea
The coin as two solid pieces: a cup below (the product) and a lid whose tongue hangs into it (the user). The cut is only the U-shaped space between them.
Why "oh nice"
Take the coin away and there is no U: the letter exists only as the gap between two pieces that fit.
What it means
The coin is made of two pieces that fit. The lower piece is the product, the upper piece is the person using it. The U for user is only the space where they meet.
Authority check
Still one round object, level and symmetrical; the gap is wide enough to read, narrow enough to stay calm.
Construction
Disc 512. Tongue 172 wide with a round foot (radius 86) at y 386; a 24-unit gap runs around it and out to the rim at y 120. Lid in the second tone, cup in the main tone. Monochrome needs nothing extra: the gap is the drawing.
Nearest existing logos
  • Tesla (a T with a curved cap)
  • Udemy (a U letter mark)
  • R2-05 the cut coin in round 2 (its own ancestor)

The idea
One notch bitten from the coin leaves a tail, and the disc becomes a speech mark. The wall of the notch is lit.
Why "oh nice"
A quotation mark is what a publication is cited with; the site would carry its own.
What it means
It is a closing quote mark, cut from a coin. A quote is what you use when you cite someone. The aim is for this site to be the thing people quote.
Authority check
A closing quote is the sign of being quoted, which is the goal. Heavy and round, it reads as editorial, not as a chat app.
Construction
Head disc radius 186; the tail is the part of a 226 circle left after a 236 notch is cut from the lower left. The lit wall is a 52-unit band along the notch. Monochrome is the silhouette alone.
Nearest existing logos
  • WhatsApp (a bubble with a tail at the lower left)
  • Disqus (a speech-bubble letter)

The idea
A heavy ring (the product) with one bead (the user) riding inside its stroke, a little wider than the track.
Why "oh nice"
The user is part of the product's loop, not orbiting outside it; the bead bulges where it does not quite fit.
What it means
The ring is the product, the loop people go round every day. The bead is the person riding in it. It is a little too big for the track, because people never quite fit the plan.
Authority check
A centered ring in a disc is an old, trusted form (a seal, a dial); the single bead keeps it from being only geometry.
Construction
Ring radius 184 to 96 (88 thick) in a circle. Bead radius 80 on the ring's center line at the upper right. The ring is the second tone; the bead is white, lighter where it sits in the stroke. Monochrome cuts an 18-unit gap around the bead.
Nearest existing logos
  • Ubuntu (dots riding a ring)
  • Target (a ring around a center)

Two heavy rings, interlocked: user and product, each passing through the other. The crossing on top is lit.

The idea
Two books: one upright, one leaning on it. Where the leaning one rests, the corner is lit.
Why "oh nice"
A shelf of a serious library, drawn with two slabs; the lean is the "oh nice".
What it means
Two books on a shelf, one leaning on the other. It is a reference library, the kind of place you go for an answer you can trust. Fifteen years of practice, put on the shelf.
Authority check
Books are the sign of a reference you can cite; the oxblood is a library binding.
Construction
In a rounded square: two books 116 by 330 (radius 18); the right one turned 24 degrees and set against the first. Monochrome cuts an 18-unit gap where they touch.

The idea
Three heavy discs, design, product and business, overlap; the place where all three meet is lit.
Why "oh nice"
The site's own subject drawn as its mark: the crossing of UX, product and business is the light in the middle.
What it means
The three circles are design, product and business. Most people work in one or two of them. This site sits in the middle, where all three cross, and that is the bright part.
Authority check
The overlap diagram is how product people already explain the job, so a professional reads it at once; heavy and symmetrical, it sits like a seal.
Construction
Three discs radius 150, centers 96 from the middle at 120-degree steps. Single areas in the main tone, the pair overlaps in the second tone, the triple overlap is the fold. Monochrome cuts the triple overlap out.
Nearest existing logos
  • the design, technology and business overlap diagram used to explain product management
  • the Ballantine three rings
  • Mastercard (two overlapping discs)

The idea
A person (head and shoulders) with one arm raised; the arm crosses the shoulder, lit where it does.
Why "oh nice"
One raised arm turns a stock figure into a person with something to ask.
What it means
Someone in the room has put their hand up with a question. Most of this site starts there: a real question from someone doing the work. The answer comes from fifteen years of having asked it too.
Authority check
Calm and level, a classroom gesture rather than a wave; reads at once as a question from the floor.
Construction
In a circle: a head radius 64 and shoulders (a dome of radius 156) running out of the disc, an arm 76 wide turned 20 degrees and crossing the shoulder. Monochrome cuts an 18-unit gap around the arm.
