Logo exploration 2: all rounds

Round 1: ten marks with weight

Ten ideas for userandproduct, 10 October 2026. Each one is judged first where the current mark failed: as a 110 px avatar in a circle and in a rounded square, and as a 32 px favicon, on white and on near-black. Then the mark at 160 px, the lockup on a thumbnail and in the header.

Foundation: authority and trust first. Every mark may have character, mass and an app-icon feel, but it must read as a serious publication a professional would cite: steady geometry, confident weight, two restrained tones, no faces, no squiggles, no splash gradients.

What we are not doing again

  • Thin lines and scattered small parts: hairline cords, rings on legs, dots on stalks (round 5's plumb line, chandelier, ring on three legs).
  • One-colour cut-outs with no depth: a figure punched through a flat disc or block (round 6's hollowed discs, round 1's imprint, round 4's sliced disc and quarter cut).
  • Abstract geometry with no readable idea: tiles, triangles, tripods and trees of three (rounds 5 and 7, including the current four-tile mark).
  • Architecture metaphors: plinths, keystones, stylobates, cornerstones, stairs, lecterns (rounds 1 to 3).
  • Letterforms and monograms: the UP shared stem, the cradle U, serif and grotesque monograms, the et ampersand: they move to round 2, a letterform round of its own.
  • Circle-and-square pairs: round 1's 'where they meet' and round 4's square and disc; nothing here is a plain circle beside a plain square.
  • Rim-breaking tiles and profiles: round 7's 'one breaks the rim' and the shortlist's profile and sphere; the one breakout here is a rocket, not a tile or a head.
  • The stock reference itself: no cursor, no arrow in a ring; only its qualities are kept: mass, one idea, a lighter fold where two forms overlap.

How to read this board

  • Container-led (7): R1-02, R1-03, R1-04, R1-05, R1-06, R1-08, R1-10. The filled circle or rounded square is part of the mark; the avatar crop is the container.
  • Stand free (3): R1-01, R1-07, R1-09. Kept so you can compare; their avatar is the mark on the page colour.
  • Colour: signal blue #2B46A0 with a pale fold (#A9BBFF) and a mid fold (#5A7BF0). R1-02 is also shown in coral, R1-07 in teal and R1-08 in oxblood.
  • Lockups keep the current Inter Display Bold wordmark, so the marks compare fairly. Letterforms and monograms are round 2.
  • Avatars and favicons are real PNG renders at the stated size, shown at one to one.

R1-01

The orbit

From the reference: ring appeal, stands free · Stands free

Idea
A thick ring (the product) with one heavy moon riding on it (the person who keeps coming back).
Oh nice
The lightest tone sits exactly where the moon crosses the ring, so the eye lands on the meeting point first.
Construction
Ring 168/98 around one centre in deep blue; moon r74 on the ring's centre line at half past one in mid blue; their intersection in the pale fold tone.
Authority check
Two plain geometric solids at rest, nothing pointing or moving; it reads like an instrument dial, not a game.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold

R1-02

The completed frame

User and product: a figure finishes the frame · Container-led: rounded square

Alternative colour: coral

Idea
A heavy screen frame with one corner missing; the person, as a solid disc, is what closes it.
Oh nice
The frame looks broken for a beat, then you see the dot is the missing corner: the product is finished by its user.
Construction
Rounded-square frame (stroke 66) with its lower-right corner cut away, in white; a disc r76 centred on that corner in pale blue; the frame ends inside the disc fold to mid blue.
Authority check
Square, centred, one rule of construction; the idea is an argument, not a joke.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold; alternative shown in coral

R1-03

The key person

Reads two ways: a key and a person · Container-led: circle

Idea
A key whose bow and guard are a head and shoulders: the user is the key to the product.
Oh nice
At a glance it is a key; a beat later the bow is a head on shoulders.
Construction
Bow ring 94/36, shaft and two bits in white; a pill guard across the shaft in pale blue; where the guard crosses shaft and bow it folds to mid blue.
Authority check
A key means access and trust; drawn upright and symmetric it reads as a seal, not a toy.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold

R1-04

The folder bust

User and product, reads two ways: a folder and a person · Container-led: rounded square

Idea
An open folder whose round tab is a head and whose back is a pair of shoulders.
Oh nice
It is a file folder until you see the person standing behind the front flap.
Construction
Back panel plus round tab in white; the slanted front flap in pale blue, wider than the back; where the flap covers the back it folds to mid blue.
Authority check
Files and records, the stuff of serious work; the person is calm and front-on, no face.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold

R1-05

The breakout rocket

Rocket, twist: it leaves the container · Container-led: rounded square

Idea
A rocket climbing out of its app tile: the nose has already crossed the edge.
Oh nice
The tone changes exactly at the tile's edge, white inside and blue outside, so the crossing is the drawing.
Construction
Rocket at 45 degrees; the hull inside the rounded square is white, the nose beyond it changes to mid blue on white (pale blue on near-black); the exhaust inside the tile is pale blue and folds to mid blue under the hull.
Authority check
One clean diagonal and an exact edge; no stars, no smoke puffs, no cartoon flame.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold

R1-06

The passenger rocket

Rocket, twist: the exhaust is the second tone, the porthole is the user · Container-led: circle

Idea
An upright rocket with one porthole, the seat of the person it is built for; its flame is the second tone.
Oh nice
The flame runs up into the hull and folds into it, so the rocket and its thrust look like one cut piece.
Construction
White hull and fins with the porthole cut through to the container; the flame teardrop in pale blue reaching up into the hull; the overlap in mid blue.
Authority check
Strictly symmetric and still, like a badge or a mission patch; no speed lines.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold

R1-08

The nib

Figurative: the author's pen · Container-led: circle

Alternative colour: oxblood

Idea
A broad pen nib: one experienced person writing; the slit folds it into a lit and a shaded face.
Oh nice
The slit and the breather hole are the only drawing, yet the nib turns three-dimensional.
Construction
Nib outline with the breather hole and slit cut through; left face white, right face pale blue; the shoulder band across the top in mid blue.
Authority check
The oldest sign of authorship; heavy and symmetric, it says 'written by someone who knows'.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold; alternative shown in oxblood

R1-10

The clasp

User and product: two forms make a third · Container-led: circle

Idea
Two open hooks that catch each other: a person and a product holding on.
Oh nice
Neither hook is closed; the grip only exists where they overlap, and that is the brightest part.
Construction
Two thick C rings (120/62) opening towards each other, the left in white, the right in pale blue; the overlaps in mid blue.
Authority check
A handshake in geometry: agreement and trust, drawn with a compass.
Colour
Blue: signal #2B46A0 with a pale fold
