Books on product management

Five books, in the order I would read them. Start with Inspired for how strong product teams work, then Escaping the Build Trap for why most companies drift away from that.

Empowered is for when you lead other PMs, and Working Backwards gives you one company's habits to copy. Product Management in Practice covers your own week. Each entry says who can skip it, so read only the ones that fit where you are now.

1 of 5 in reading order

Inspired

Marty Cagan, second edition, 2017

What it covers
How the best product companies decide what to build. Cagan argues that a product team should test ideas with real customers before engineers build them, and should be judged by what changes for customers rather than by the number of features shipped. He also sets out what a product manager owns.
Who it is for
Every product manager, new or senior. Designers and engineering leads read it to learn what to expect from their PM.
Who can skip it
PMs who already test ideas with customers every week and have read it once. A second read gives less than the next book here.
The part that matters most
The chapters on product discovery: testing an idea cheaply, with prototypes and real users, before it reaches the backlog.
Where it has aged
Still current. Get the second edition (2017), not the 2008 first edition.

2 of 5 in reading order

Escaping the Build Trap

Melissa Perri, 2018

What it covers
The build trap is a company that measures itself by what it ships instead of what changes for customers. Perri shows how teams fall into it and how they climb out: set outcomes, give product managers room to decide, and connect the company's strategy to the work of each team.
Who it is for
PMs and leaders in a feature factory: a company where the roadmap is a list of promised features and nobody checks what they did.
Who can skip it
Teams that already plan around outcomes and review them every quarter. You will recognize most of it.
The part that matters most
The chapters on strategy: how one company goal becomes goals that each team can act on.
Where it has aged
Still current.

Not yet read by the author; on the shelf because Lenny's Newsletter, Mind the Product and Giff Constable all recommend it.

Read it with

  • Empowered: what its leaders do differently

3 of 5 in reading order

Empowered

Marty Cagan and Chris Jones, 2020

What it covers
The companion to Inspired, written for the people who lead product teams. It sets feature teams, which are handed a list of things to build, against empowered teams, which are handed a problem and trusted to solve it. Most of the book is the leader's side: coaching, hiring, and a vision and strategy the teams can act on.
Who it is for
Heads of product, directors, and anyone who manages product managers.
Who can skip it
A PM in the first year with nobody reporting to them. Read Inspired now and come back to this when you lead people.
The part that matters most
The chapters on coaching product managers, a part of the job most leaders were never taught.
Where it has aged
Still current.

Not yet read by the author; on the shelf because Lenny's Newsletter, Mind the Product and Productboard all recommend it.

Read it with
  • Inspired: read it first
  • Working Backwards: one company's habits, written down

4 of 5 in reading order

Working Backwards

Colin Bryar and Bill Carr, 2021

What it covers
Two former Amazon executives describe the habits Amazon runs on. The best known is writing the press release, and the questions a customer would ask, before anything gets built. The book also covers six-page memos in place of slide decks, leaders who own one thing full time, and the numbers that move before revenue does.
Who it is for
PMs and leaders who want working habits they can copy, with the reason behind each one.
Who can skip it
Readers looking for a theory of product management. These are one company's practices, and a few depend on Amazon's size.
The part that matters most
The chapters on the press release and the six-page memo. They change how a team writes before it builds.
Where it has aged
Still current.

Not yet read by the author; on the shelf because it is in SVPG's top ten product books and on Lenny's Newsletter's essential list.

Read it with

5 of 5 in reading order

Product Management in Practice

Matt LeMay, second edition, 2022

What it covers
The day-to-day job as people live it, not as job ads describe it. LeMay puts communication first: most of a PM's work is getting the right people to understand the same thing. He groups the rest under organization, research and execution.
Who it is for
New and aspiring PMs, and anyone moving into the role from design, engineering or support.
Who can skip it
Senior PMs who already run the job well. The book names good habits; you will have most of them.
The part that matters most
The chapters on communication, with plain advice for the meetings and messages that go wrong.
Where it has aged
Still current. Get the second edition (2022).

Not yet read by the author; on the shelf because Mind the Product and Productboard both recommend it.

Read it with
  • How to Write a PRD That Engineers Actually Read: a document the whole team reads
  • Inspired: the bigger picture of the role
