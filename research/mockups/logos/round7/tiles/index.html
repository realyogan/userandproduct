Logo concepts, round seven: four tiles

Eighteen marks from the owner's sketch of four rounded rectangles turning around a center: filled, outlined, and carved out of a disc the way Tiger Data folds its tiger into one. Every concept starts from one layout, the pinwheel tiling (four tiles, each turned a quarter from the last), and varies the turn, the gap, the corners, the tile proportion and the treatment. Each sits beside one settled lowercase wordmark in Instrument Sans or Fraunces. Pick one.

Each concept is shown at 560px on its paper and on near-black, then in a 1280px site header strip on white and on near-black, then the mark, the favicon art with its 32px and 16px renders, one color, the avatar and a profile tile. Below that, the three closest existing logos from the image check, and the notes. Pin any lockup to compare.

How originality was checked. About ninety logo files were downloaded, rendered on contact sheets and looked at, not only searched by description: Microsoft, the Windows 11 and Windows 8 flags, Slack, Chase, Google Photos, Dropbox, Binance, Zoho, Kentico, Trello, Mitsubishi, Columbia, Toyota, Audi, Mastercard, Airtable, Buffer, Tiger Data, Linear, Craft CMS, Deutsche Bank, the sun cross, and marks from software, consulting, finance and publishing (Accenture, American Express, Barclays, Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, Visa, Revolut, Monzo, O'Reilly, Pearson, Elsevier, Medium, Substack and others).

The rule for turning. Every tilt was checked for a hooked cross or a sun cross, in the tiles and in the space between them. Tiles stay whole and separate; carved discs keep their cuts inside 39 percent of the radius (except the one tile that breaks the rim in S12); no straight cross of space ever runs through a ring. A converging version, where the tiles close in until the gaps form a cross of space, was not built: straight, it is Microsoft or a sun cross; jogged, it is a hooked cross.

Filled: S1 turning square, S2 the sketch, S3 quilt block, S4 open frame, S5 two sizes, S6 the user among the product (accent).

Outlined: S7 outlined sketch, S8 four diamonds, S9 mixed weights.

Carved: S10 pierced disc, S11 inlaid disc, S12 one breaks the rim, S13 carved block (square), S14 three cut and one kept, S18 window disc.

Derivatives: S15 four pages fanned, S16 two tiles, S17 the empty seat.

Top three by the masthead test

  1. S14 Three cut, one kept: the only concept that says something: three tiles cut, the user left standing in red. Navy and one red is a journal palette, the disc gives the Tiger Data seal, and in one color it still works because the fourth tile simply stays uncut.
  2. S2 The sketch: the owner's own drawing, made exact. Ink on cream with Fraunces looks like a printer's ornament on a title page: the most publication-like and the least like a software icon.
  3. S10 The pierced disc: the calmest seal: a solid navy disc pierced by four turned tiles, small enough that it never reads as a ring and a cross. It would sit on a report cover or a conference lanyard without explanation, and it is the strongest at 16px.

Dropped, and why

Plain grid, square-onFour equal squares on a straight grid, sharp or rounded: Microsoft, the Windows 11 flag and every app-grid icon. One change at most.
Grid turned 45 degreesThe same grid turned on its point: Microsoft turned, and one step from Binance and the Dropbox diamonds.
Squares spun in placeFour squares square-on, each spun 18 degrees (first S1): at 16px it still read as Microsoft.
1:2 pills round a holeLong tiles in the pinwheel leave a square hole the width of a tile: the Chase octagon, and it spun like a windmill at small sizes.
Tiles through the rimAll four tiles cut out through the edge of the disc (nine sizes tried): the solid left between them is a hooked cross. Discarded outright.
Tiles filling the discLarge floating cuts leave a thin ring and a thin cross: a sun cross. Every carved disc now keeps its tiles inside 39 percent of the radius.
A hand of cardsFour sheets fanned from the bottom center (first S15): a poker hand, the wrong register. Now fanned from one corner like a swatch book.

RationaleThe pinwheel tiling at its plainest: four 7:5 tiles, each turned a quarter from the last, so the four together make one square that seems to turn. Turned 18 degrees off the page so it rests rather than spins. Soft corners keep it from looking like a floor plan.
